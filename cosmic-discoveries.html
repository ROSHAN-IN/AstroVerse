Galaxies

Our home has spiral arms.

The Milky Way is a barred spiral galaxy. Our solar system sits in its disk, far from the galactic centre.

Black Holes

A boundary light cannot escape.

A black hole's event horizon marks a boundary beyond which light cannot escape to distant observers.

Stars

Starlight begins with fusion.

During the main stage of their lives, stars produce energy by fusing hydrogen into helium in their cores.

Nebulae

Some clouds build new stars.

Nebulae are clouds of gas and dust in space. Some contain regions where gravity gathers material into new stars.

Comets

A comet can grow two tails.

Near the Sun, a comet can develop a dust tail and an ion tail. The ion tail points away from the Sun because of the solar wind.

Asteroids

Remnants of the solar system's beginnings.

Asteroids are remnants from the early solar system. Most orbit in the main asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.

Exoplanets

Distant worlds can reveal themselves in shadows.

Astronomers can detect some exoplanets by measuring the small drop in a star's brightness when a planet passes in front of it.
